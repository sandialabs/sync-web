\chapter{Application Sketches}
\label{sec:applications}
\label{sec:worked-example}

The following sketches test the architecture against domains with different institutions, retention horizons, and consequences. They are not claims of completed applications. The first section follows one inspection report through a conceptual end-to-end flow. Local commit, slicing, and proof mechanisms ground the example; reciprocal synchronization, compiled federation, and response binding include proposed WIP stages. Later sections vary the institutions and retention problem without repeating every mechanism.

\section{Worked Evidence Flow}

Consider a manufacturer M that records an inspection report for component 17 in its ordinary quality-management database. The scenario extends manufacturing traceability models~\cite{Stouffer2022traceability} and secure provenance across organizations~\cite{Hasan2009provenance} with independently retained histories. A projection worker deterministically encodes the report and places it at a stable journal-relative path such as \texttt{components/17/inspection}. The path names the continuing resource, while the document or object stored there determines the bytes, metadata, and interpretation of this version. M stages the write together with declared transition metadata. At the next step, the journal compares the staged state with its prior head, records the supplied time, appends the new state, constructs the canonical pre-signature view, signs it, and advances M's root. Call the resulting signed head $M_i$.

\paragraph{Selective evidence.} A buyer B rarely needs M's complete state. M therefore slices $M_i$ along the inspection path. Unrelated branches become stubs, but the slice retains the same root digest. B can verify the report, object definition, and path against $M_i$ and later merge compatible evidence without changing the identity of the state. If M prunes its own complete copy after the retention window, the commitment remains meaningful but future retrieval depends on some participant having retained the report.

\paragraph{Historical incorporation.} Suppose M and B maintain a reciprocal bridge. Synchronization verifies and stages $M_i$ at B; it does not yet prove that B committed it. When B later advances to $B_j$ with $M_i$ included beneath its bridge state, $B_j$ and an inclusion proof become M's incorporation receipt. The ordering claim is limited but durable: the inspection state represented by $M_i$ existed before $B_j$. B may pin the inspection slice in its retained history, while M and B continue advancing independently.

\paragraph{Private resolution.} If the report remains protected at M, the WIP federated design first compiles endpoint-relative route proofs from the exact heads and nested checkpoints retained along B's route to M. B learns the M identity and endpoint that B can verify from its own state; M receives a self-contained proof of the B interface key that will sign the request. B then sends the private resolve directly to M over authenticated secure transport, bypassing intermediaries. M authenticates the B-relative principal and applies terminal-local authorization. Once the WIP response-binding format is specified, M returns report evidence bound to the handshake-selected M head, and B's client verifies it before exposing the resource as ordinary application data.

\paragraph{Correction and disagreement.} A later correction creates a successor state $M_{i+1}$ rather than rewriting $M_i$. B may retain both, accept the correction, contest it in B's own history, or decline further synchronization. Integrity preserves which versions and cross-history orderings were recorded. It does not determine whether the component passed inspection or whether the physical component presented to B is component 17. The workflow is therefore complete only when cryptographic evidence is combined with physical identifiers, inspection practice, and institutional judgment.

\section{Persistent Web Archives}

Web resources can be projected into independently operated journals as durable, historically versioned state. Multiple archives may preserve overlapping portions of a site's history and entangle their own histories without depending on one archival operator. Root-relative paths identify both resource lineage and a particular capture while the source system continues serving current traffic. A static site, content-management system, or database can remain load-bearing as an asynchronous projection adds evidence; selected projected resources may assume a larger operational role later, without an all-at-once migration.

Long-term storage research warns that unlikely failures and cryptographic aging become ordinary over decades~\cite{Chun2009tiered}. Archives therefore still need capture-fidelity, privacy, legal-erasure, malicious-content, media-renewal, retention, and migration policies. Historical integrity makes alteration detectable relative to retained commitments; it does not operate the archive indefinitely.

\section{Portable Identity Anchors}

A person can establish a stable path beneath a selected well-known root and use it to identify a profile hosted by a replaceable journal. Moving the profile becomes a historical transition instead of abandoning every reference: observers resolve the old relationship, inspect the redirecting state, and apply their own trust policy. Historic name trails provide the close precedent of provider-controlled names leading to later user-directed identifiers~\cite{Maniatis2003hints}; journal-relative history extends that pattern to richer state and reciprocal relationships.

Portability reduces dependence on one host, but it does not remove the need for anchor governance, key recovery, impersonation defenses, endpoint discovery, privacy, or continued availability. A stable path is useful only to observers who accept its root and can obtain enough history to verify the transition.

\section{Agent Reputation Discovery}

A user can choose journals or communities as starting roots and discover reporting agents through explicit relationships instead of accepting one platform ranking. Reports, cited sources, corrections, and later outcomes become locally resolvable evidence, allowing communities to retain different records and reach different judgments. Secure per-node logs and deterministic replay offer a related accountability model~\cite{Haeberlen2007peerreview}; partially retained resource history additionally keeps the evidence behind one evaluation identifiable after the ranking or agent changes.

Durable history cannot eliminate Sybil identities, collusion, compromised keys, selective reporting, or repeated falsehoods. It can make the basis of local judgment inspectable and make later correction distinguishable from retrospective rewriting. Institutions and users still decide which roots, witnesses, and evaluation rules deserve weight.

\section{Common Pattern}

Each sketch begins with independently administered operational state. Selected resources receive stable root-relative coordinates, local commitments preserve successive states, and explicit relationships connect histories across institutions. Partial evidence permits selective retention, self-encoding structure keeps durable interpretation associated with the state, and local policy decides which remote identities and operations carry authority.

The recurring value is not universal agreement. It is the ability to retain and compare evidence across institutions and time without requiring those institutions to share one operator, schema, or global sequence.
