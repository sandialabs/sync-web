The Web gives people extraordinary reach but rarely gives them durable, independently verifiable relationships to the state and history on which that reach depends. Resources remain subject to the mutable databases, interfaces, and institutional memory of their current operators. This paper presents the Synchronic Web as an architecture for strengthening human agency, decentralized reputation, and collective work with trustworthy information without requiring one platform owner or one global history. Its central thesis is that a Web can be formed from self-encoding, partially knowable, historically composable state with verifiable integrity. A minimal integrity substrate makes partial structure independently checkable; durable definitions travel with the state they interpret; journals preserve independently administered histories; and reciprocal bridges compose those histories through the established principle of timeline entanglement. Journal-relative paths then make ordinary resources reachable from plural local viewpoints. The contribution is not a new claim for cross-history entanglement itself, but an architectural synthesis of entangled history, proof-preserving partial possession, self-encoding interpretation, resource paths, and local authority. The resulting design narrows dependence on remote operators while explicitly retaining the burdens of availability, authorization, migration, discovery, resource control, and social judgment.
