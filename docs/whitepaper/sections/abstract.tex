The Web gives people extraordinary reach but rarely gives them durable, independently verifiable relationships to the state and history on which that reach depends. Resources remain subject to the mutable databases, interfaces, and institutional memory of their current operators. This paper presents the Synchronic Web as an architecture for strengthening human agency and collective work with trustworthy information without requiring one platform owner or one global history. Its central thesis is that a Web can be formed from self-encoding, partially knowable, historically composable state with verifiable integrity. A minimal integrity substrate makes partial structure independently checkable, while durable definitions travel with the state they interpret. Reciprocal bridges exchange verified heads; later local commits that incorporate those heads entangle independently administered histories according to an established secure-timeline principle. Journal-relative paths then make ordinary resources reachable from plural local viewpoints. The contribution is this architectural synthesis, not a new claim for cross-history entanglement itself. It narrows dependence on remote operators while leaving availability, authorization, migration, and social judgment outside cryptographic integrity.
