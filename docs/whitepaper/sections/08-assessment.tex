\section{Bounded Guarantees}

The Synchronic Web builds assurance from explicit commitments. An accepted root and correct verifier establish structural integrity for available evidence; a signature authenticates the key that authorized a head; historical resolution identifies earlier states; entanglement orders a foreign head before a later incorporating head; an exact committed route object connects a remote key and endpoint to evidence retained by the relying journal; and authorization applies local rules to the resulting principal. These layers answer different questions and depend on distinct operations: authentication gives a key historical meaning, domain evidence supports truth, replication and preservation sustain availability, witness diversity exposes divergent branches, and selective disclosure, terminal policy, and secure transport protect confidentiality. The local client, evaluator, runtime, and key store form the trusted computing base through which these assurances reach an application. \Cref{tab:assurance-matrix} summarizes each mechanism and its operational conditions. The bounded character of these guarantees is a source of clarity: a digest can remain valid after every full copy disappears and thereby identify a preservation failure, two valid signatures can expose an equivocation, and a deterministic transition can reproduce the effect of a supplied timestamp while leaving its accuracy to the source. The architecture preserves evidence for judgment and keeps the evidentiary boundary visible.

\section{Costs and Architectural Fit}

Integrity adds hashing, persisted handles, proof paths, and verification work; history adds retained states and key stewardship; partial possession saves content storage while keeping ancestor evidence, stumps, and serialization metadata; self-encoding objects add evaluation, confinement, debugging, compatibility, and migration; and federation adds exact-object routing, direct transport, terminal-local authorization, origin verification of historical results, and application handling of accepted replay. These costs make architectural fit important, as summarized in \cref{tab:architectural-fit}. The Synchronic Web becomes compelling when independent roots, selective possession, durable interpretation, historical correction, and cross-institutional evidence coincide, as they may in production traceability, portable identity, or decentralized archives. Applications may still add coordination above the journal layer for payments, settlement, exclusive allocation, deadlines, or physical custody; journals give these domain protocols durable inputs and histories, while each application defines its shared decision procedure.

\section{Technical Path Forward}

The records layer now covers reciprocal continuity, exact-object multi-hop routing, directional aliases, signed descriptor/key/endpoint/audience binding, direct \texttt{get}/\texttt{set!}/\texttt{resolve}, terminal-local authorization, and origin verification and retention of resolved proofs. \WIP The work ahead should follow dependency---integrity and history invariants first, federation and authorization next, then general semantic and client surfaces---and connect digest/handle separation, root advancement, partial possession, strict evaluation, bridge incorporation, and terminal-local authorization directly to implementing code and tests. Four technical fronts lead from this foundation to a durable specification:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Federation:} exercise real TLS; define endpoint cutover beyond operational overlap or forwarding; constrain auto-accept egress and SSRF exposure; retrieve incorporation receipts; develop broader durable response receipts where applications require them; and implement the stable journal identifier and predecessor-authorized signing-key lineage.
  \item \textbf{Execution:} mediate ambient time; meter computation, allocation, recursion, reads, results, and mutation; and test application-defined objects and fixed-head read queries through persistence, proof, authorization, migration, rollback, and touched-data evidence.
  \item \textbf{Resources:} specify projection manifests, correction, privacy, deletion, backpressure, bidirectional synchronization, and rebuildable derived indices.
  \item \textbf{Long-horizon assurance:} develop authenticated key recovery, interpreter compatibility, cryptographic renewal, formal models, property tests, and workload measurements for history growth, proof size, route traversal, projection, object execution, and queries.
\end{itemize}

\section{Toward a Plural Web}

The architecture begins from a modest observation: durable information needs both continuity and a point of view. Integrity-protected state, signed succession, retained definitions, and cross-history receipts supply continuity; a journal that chooses roots, preserves evidence, follows relationships, and applies local policy supplies the viewpoint. Their composition produces a distinctive Web in which resources survive changes in representation and host, histories intersect while retaining independent order, observers possess different portions of one committed state, definitions evolve beside the objects they interpret, and federated paths preserve the historical routes through which other journals became meaningful. This is the deeper value of timeline entanglement in a resource architecture~\cite{Maniatis2002timeline}: foreign history becomes locally retained evidence, self-encoding structure turns evidence into durable meaning, journal-relative paths make that meaning findable and citable, and specialized clients keep the machinery quiet during ordinary use and explicit during inspection. A plural Web will still contain disagreement, loss, compromise, and institutional change; its strength lies in giving those events durable form through corrections, receipts, witnesses, and independently held roots. If the remaining protocol and implementation work succeeds, networked information can become easier to carry across time while preserving the diversity of systems and institutions that give it meaning.
